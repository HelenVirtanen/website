MoscowCSS grew out of PiterCSS from Saint Petersburg. We wanted to focus the conversation on interfaces: the big industry conferences mostly talked about JavaScript, good interfaces were rare, and accessibility had its problems too. Web technologies were only just finding their feet back then, and that needed talking about.

The second goal was to give juniors a platform. Most conferences were expensive and attracted very experienced developers. We wanted an event that develops the community and helps people grow.

The third goal, no less important, was a stage for first-time speakers. The meetups were free, and we often put a top speaker and people speaking for the first time in the same programme. For many, MoscowCSS became their first stage: for Andrey Smirnov, for example, it was one of his first talks. Over the years the meetup has raised more than one great speaker. We also showcased the community, companies and venues.

Today MoscowCSS is one of the main IT events in Moscow. I'm glad we started it back then, and even more glad that others picked it up and it is still alive. Usually, when the founders step away from an event, it disappears. That didn't happen with MoscowCSS, and that's really cool. The industry has changed a lot since then, and we keep talking about interfaces just as we did. That matters.

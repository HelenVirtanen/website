### My path through moscss: from the first "wow" to a talk of my own

You know how some events you look forward to like a holiday? For me, that event became the moscss meetup. It wasn't always that way, though: at first it was plain curiosity, which grew into something bigger each time.

I came to moscss for the first time by accident. I saw an announcement in my feed and decided to drop in. I remember walking into the hall and feeling literally lost: so many people brought together by a field I cared about, so many engaged faces, such a lively exchange of experience and knowledge. That evening I sat in the back row, soaking up every word and thinking: "This is it. This is where I truly belong." I didn't know yet that it was only the beginning.

Over time I noticed the programme getting richer with every meetup. The talks went deeper, the discussions got hotter, and after the official part came informal conversations that were just as valuable. And I kept making new acquaintances. At first we just said hello, then we started discussing the talks, and then sharing our tasks and finding common ground.

Little by little, I got drawn into the field. What once seemed distant and difficult became clear and familiar. Most importantly, the new connections began to bear fruit. Thanks to people I met at moscss, I found a job. It wasn't just employment, it was landing in an environment where I feel I belong.

One day I made up my mind to give a talk myself. I was terribly nervous, but the community's support did its work. When I stood on stage and saw interested eyes, questions from the audience, a live response, I realised: this is incredibly cool. Sharing what you know, getting feedback, being part of a common cause is a feeling like no other.

Now, every time news of a new meetup comes out, I look forward to it like a holiday. Because moscss isn't just meetings. It's people who have become colleagues and friends. It's the environment I grew up in. It's the place where something important happens every time, both for me and for the community.
